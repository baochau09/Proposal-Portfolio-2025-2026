Mental Models

How I think before I plan

Three lenses I return to every time I read a brief — tools for cutting through noise and finding the right question first.

Pillar 01

Breadth × Depth Awareness

A brand can be widely known but still not surface at the moment of purchase. Two different problems — two different solutions.

In use: Map where a brand sits on the Breadth–Depth matrix before recommending media weight.
Pillar 02

ELM + Cognitive Load

The Elaboration Likelihood Model tells us when logic works and when peripheral cues work better.

In use: High-consideration → central route. Impulse → sensory hooks and visual rhythm.
Pillar 03

Systematize the Mechanism

Good strategy should be repeatable. I break results into named drivers so wins can be rebuilt — not just celebrated.

In use: Document which signal, format, or placement drove uplift. Turns wins into transferable logic.

Cross-Category Strategic Patterns

The same models,
different categories

Not category knowledge alone. Repeatable mental models that diagnose very different growth problems.

Breadth × Depth Awareness

Widely known does not mean chosen at the right moment. Most brands mistake one problem for the other.
Education

Broad reach fills the funnel. Enrollment is won through depth — consistent signal, friction removal, across a 3–6 month cycle.

Derma Beauty

Bioderma had awareness. The gap was CEP-linked salience — surfacing at the exact moment of suncare need.

Fashion / H&E

Media feeds the activation ecosystem. Its job: deliver warm audiences to livestreams. Breadth without infrastructure is waste.

First question: Reach problem or salience problem? The answer changes everything downstream.
Reach × Salience Matrix

ELM + Cognitive Load

Some categories reward argument. Others punish it. Getting this wrong means paying for logic when you needed a cue.
Healthcare / Derma

Central route. HCP endorsement, clinical proof, education before trial. Trust must be earned.

FMCG

Peripheral route. Sensory hooks, visual rhythm, character cues. Aww, CAT! was emotional encoding for a 3-second scroll.

Education / Services

High cognitive load. Strategy is subtraction: remove friction, simplify sequencing. Clarity is the brief.

First question: Does more information help — or create abandonment?
Dual-Route Persuasion

Identify the Growth Lever

Before channels or creative: find the one mechanism that actually produces growth — then build everything to serve it.
Trial → Bioderma

Sampling was the growth lever. The entire media plan reorganized around making trial desirable and visible.

Habit → bTaskee

Gateway habit formation. Cleaning unlocked multi-service adoption. Campaign reinforced the loop, not services.

Frequency → CISS

Localized high-frequency dominance within a defined enrollment calendar. The Goldfish Model was a named lever.

First question: What is the one thing that, if it worked, would make everything else work?
Growth Lever → Mechanism → Output
